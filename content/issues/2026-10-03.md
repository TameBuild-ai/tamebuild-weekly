**Tame the Week: October 3, 2026**

Three things that happened in AI this week, and what each one means if you run a shop, an office or a few rentals.

**1. A cloned voice talked a bank chairman out of €95 million**

In Italy, the chairman of Fideuram, a private bank owned by Intesa Sanpaolo, got a WhatsApp message that looked like it came from his CEO. Then came a phone call from what sounded like the company's outside lawyer, except the voice was made with AI. He approved transfers totaling €95 million, and about €36 million is still missing.

*What it means for you:* If it can fool a bank chairman, it can fool your bookkeeper. Set one rule this week: any request to move money or change payment details gets confirmed by calling the person back on a number you already have, never the one in the message. Microsoft's annual security report, out Friday, found phishing was how attackers got in on 23% of the break-ins it investigated, up from 7% a year earlier.

Sources: [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/news/ai-voice-scam-hits-italian-bank-for-95-million/), [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/02/ai-cybersecurity-threats-microsoft-report/)

**2. Meta wants an AI assistant running your back office**

Meta launched Muse for Small Business, which hooks its AI assistant into Shopify, Stripe, QuickBooks, Canva, Slack, your Facebook Page, Instagram and your Meta ad account. Most people can use it free, and Meta says it won't post, send messages or buy anything without your approval.

*What it means for you:* A capable AI assistant is now free and a click away for a typical small business. It can save real time drafting posts or pulling sales numbers. Connect one account first, watch what it does with it, and add more only once you trust it.

Source: [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/meta-muse-small-business-ai-agent)

**3. Apple is tightening what AI apps can see on your Mac**

Apple said Mac apps will need a very explicit sign-off before they get Full Disk Access, the setting that lets software read everything on your computer. Apple named AI agents as the reason, pointing to a report that Meta's Muse read a user's private messages (Meta disputes it) and a security flaw in ChatGPT's Mac app.

*What it means for you:* Check what you've already allowed. On a Mac, open System Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Full Disk Access, and switch off anything you don't recognize or no longer use. Customer files and tax records on that machine are exactly what these tools can reach.

Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/02/apple-says-its-tightening-macos-full-disk-access-controls-due-to-new-risks-from-ai-agents/)
