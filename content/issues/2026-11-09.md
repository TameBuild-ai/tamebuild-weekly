**Tame the Week: November 9, 2026**

A federal case this week shows how convincing a fake boss can be, and two other changes touch your Maps listing and your Microsoft bill.

**1. Prosecutors say AI fake bosses and fake lawyers took $129 million from companies**

Prosecutors charged three men with a scheme that allegedly used AI to imitate the voices and images of CEOs and of lawyers at major law firms. CyberScoop reports it typically started with accounting staff and an email asking them to sign a nondisclosure agreement over a "confidential financial transaction" before contacting a fake attorney. Prosecutors say the crew picked employees able to approve large wire transfers, and a Florida pet food company lost $5.2 million. These are allegations, and one defendant's lawyers deny involvement.

What it means for you: This week, set one rule for whoever pays your bills. No wire, ACH payment or change of bank details goes out because of an email, text or call alone, even when the voice sounds like your boss or your lawyer. Call back on a number you already had on file, and have a second person approve anything above an amount you pick. Treat any demand for secrecy before a payment as a stop sign.

Source: [CyberScoop, Nov. 6, 2026](https://cyberscoop.com/ai-deepfakes-impersonate-ceo-lawyer-defraud-companies)

**2. Google Maps now shows a Message button next to Call on listings with chat**

Google's November 5 Small Business Bulletin says businesses that have set up a chat option on their Business Profile now show a "Message" button next to "Call" on Google Maps, on both Android and iOS. Search Engine Journal reports that businesses may give customers a WhatsApp link for those messages. It also points out that a dead link or an unread inbox now sits in one of the top buttons on your listing.

What it means for you: Look up your business on Google Maps on your phone this week. If you see Message, send yourself a test and time how long a reply takes. Decide who checks that inbox and how often, and aim to answer the same day. If the button points to a WhatsApp number nobody watches, fix the link in your Business Profile before more customers tap it. If you see no Message button, nothing changes on your listing for now.

Source: [Search Engine Journal, Nov. 6, 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-gemini-business-profile-workspace-2/596120/)

**3. Microsoft pushes back usage-based Copilot Business billing through partners to early 2027**

Microsoft had planned to turn on usage-based billing by default for new eligible Copilot Business subscriptions bought through its Cloud Solution Provider program starting December 1, 2026. NeoTeo reports that a November 3 notice moved that to January through March 2027. The announced default limit is 4,000 Copilot Credits per licensed user per month, with no dollar amount given. Microsoft says a new qualifying purchase will also turn usage-based billing on by default for existing Copilot Business licenses in the same tenant.

What it means for you: If you pay for Microsoft 365 Copilot through an IT company or reseller, send them a short email this week. Ask whether your licenses are bought through that program, what happens to your bill when the change starts, and who sets the spending limit. Hold off on adding Copilot seats or buying a bundle until they answer, since a new purchase is what triggers the change. If you buy directly from Microsoft, the report does not say you are covered.

Source: [NeoTeo, Nov. 5, 2026](https://www.neoteo.com/en/microsoft-delays-planned-copilot-business-csp-usage-billing-to-fy27-q3)
