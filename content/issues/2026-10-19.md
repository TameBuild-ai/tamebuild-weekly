**Tame the Week: October 19, 2026**

Three things changed this week for Gmail users, restaurants and counter-service shops.

**1. Gmail now has a voice search for your inbox**

Google is rolling out Gmail Live to paid Workspace accounts on Android and iPhone. You ask questions out loud and hear spoken answers pulled from your own inbox, and each answer shows a card pointing to the email it came from. The rollout started October 15 and can take up to 15 days to appear. For now it is only for users in the US with EN-US as their language.

What it means for you: Google says the feature will be on by default for organizations with Gemini in Workspace. If you run Workspace for your business, decide this week whether you want it. The Google post links an admin help page called "Turn Gemini features on or off for users," so start there. If you keep it on, try it yourself first. Tap Live in the Gmail search bar on your phone and allow microphone access when asked. Ask about a supplier order, then open the source card and check the email before you act on the answer. Tell your staff it reads from the inbox, so they should not use it with the speaker on in front of customers.

Source: [Google Workspace Updates: Gmail Live is rolling out to paid Workspace users](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/gmail-live-is-rolling-out-to-paid-Workspace-users.html)

**2. Google added more diet labels for restaurant menus**

Google is rolling out new dietary attributes for restaurants in Google Business Profiles. Gluten-free, dairy-free, halal and kosher join the vegetarian and vegan tags that were already there. Lisa Landsman from Google said that when diners search for specific diets, like "gluten-free dinner near me," tagged dishes help your items stand out on your profile.

What it means for you: If you own a restaurant, spend 20 minutes on this. Sign in to your Google Business Profile, select Edit menu, choose an item, and check the dietary badges that apply. Start with your ten best sellers. Only tag a dish if your kitchen can stand behind it, because a wrong gluten-free tag is a bigger problem than no tag.

Source: [Search Engine Roundtable: New Google Local Dietary Attributes](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-business-profiles-dietary-attributes-42281.html)

**3. Square launched a talking self-order kiosk, with prices**

Square unveiled a kiosk with a voice assistant that knows your menu. Customers can ask about the menu, request changes and work through substitutions. It is available now for $1,999, which includes a counter stand, and it requires a Square Plus or Premium subscription. Square also lists a $99 monthly software fee per device. Plus is $49 per location per month, and Premium is $149.

What it means for you: This is aimed at quick-service restaurants, so skip it if that isn't you. If it is, do the math before a salesperson does it for you. One kiosk is $1,999 up front plus $1,188 a year in software fees, so at least $3,187 in the first year before the plan fee. Compare that with the hours of counter labor it would cover during your rush. Square's own release says the system can address menu questions about allergy information. If you try it, check that your menu and ingredient details are accurate first.

Source: [Square: Introducing the New Square Kiosk](https://squareup.com/us/en/press/square-kiosk-self-service-reimagined)
